Conservation of Plants and Animals. A wide variety of plants and animals live on Earth, and they are essential for human well-being and survival. Biodiversity, or biological diversity, refers to the variety of organisms existing on Earth, their interrelationships and their relationship with the environment. Today the survival of many organisms is threatened by human activities.

Deforestation means clearing forests and using the land for other purposes. Forests are cut for procuring land for cultivation, building houses and factories, and making furniture or using wood as fuel. Natural causes such as forest fires and severe droughts also lead to deforestation. Deforestation has many consequences. It increases the temperature and pollution level on Earth, and it increases the level of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, because fewer trees are left to use carbon dioxide during photosynthesis. Ground water level gets lowered and the natural balance is disturbed. Deforestation can lead to droughts and floods, and it reduces rainfall.

Deforestation also changes the properties of the soil. Plants hold the soil with their roots; when trees are removed, the top layer of soil is exposed and is easily washed away by wind and water. This removal of the top layer of soil is called soil erosion. Soil erosion reduces fertility, because the fertile top layer is lost, and the hard and rocky lower layers have less humus. If deforestation continues, fertile land can gradually be converted into a desert. This is called desertification.

To protect our flora (plants) and fauna (animals) and their habitats, protected areas called wildlife sanctuaries, national parks and biosphere reserves have been earmarked. Plantation, cultivation, grazing, felling trees, hunting and poaching are prohibited in these areas.

A wildlife sanctuary is an area where animals are protected from any disturbance to them and their habitat. A national park is an area reserved for wildlife where animals can freely use the habitats and natural resources; national parks are large and diverse enough to protect whole sets of ecosystems. Satpura National Park in Madhya Pradesh is the first reserve forest of India. A biosphere reserve is a large area of protected land for the conservation of wildlife, plant and animal resources and the traditional life of the tribals living in the area. Pachmarhi Biosphere Reserve in Madhya Pradesh includes Satpura National Park and the Bori and Pachmarhi wildlife sanctuaries. A biosphere reserve may contain other protected areas within it.

Plants and animals found in a particular area are termed the flora and fauna of that area. Species that are found exclusively in a particular area are called endemic species; they are not naturally found anywhere else. A particular type of animal or plant may be endemic to a zone, a state or a country. Sal and wild mango are endemic flora of the Pachmarhi Biosphere Reserve, and the bison, Indian giant squirrel and flying squirrel are endemic fauna of that area. Destruction of their habitat, increasing population and the introduction of new species can affect the natural habitat of endemic species and endanger their existence.

A species is a group of populations that are capable of interbreeding; members of a species can reproduce fertile offspring only with members of their own species. Animals whose numbers are diminishing to a level that they might face extinction are known as endangered animals. The dinosaurs, which lived on Earth millions of years ago, became extinct. Small animals are in greater danger than big animals in some cases, because they can be killed easily and their habitats can be destroyed without anyone noticing. Project Tiger was launched by the government to protect tigers in the country; its objective was to ensure the survival and maintenance of the tiger population. The tiger is one of the many species that are slowly disappearing from our forests.

The Red Data Book is the source book that keeps a record of all the endangered animals and plants. It is maintained by an international organisation, and India also maintains a Red Data Book for the plants and animals found in India.

Migration is the phenomenon of movement of a species from its own habitat to some other habitat for a particular time period every year, for a particular purpose such as breeding. Migratory birds fly to faraway areas every year during a particular time because of climatic changes; they fly to lay eggs because the weather in their natural habitat becomes very cold and inhospitable.

Paper is made from wood, and it takes about 17 full-grown trees to make one tonne of paper, so we should save, reuse and recycle paper. Paper can be recycled five to seven times for use. Reforestation is the restocking of destroyed forests by planting new trees. The planted trees should generally be of the same species that were found in that forest, and we should plant at least as many trees as we cut. Reforestation can also take place naturally if a deforested area is left undisturbed. In India, the Forest (Conservation) Act aims at the preservation and conservation of natural forests and at meeting the basic needs of the people living in or near the forests.
