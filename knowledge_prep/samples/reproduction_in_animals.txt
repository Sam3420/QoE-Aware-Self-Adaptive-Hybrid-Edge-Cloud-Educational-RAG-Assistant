Reproduction in Animals. Reproduction is the process by which organisms produce young ones of their own kind. It is essential for the continuation of a species. There are two modes of reproduction in animals: sexual reproduction and asexual reproduction.

Reproduction resulting from the fusion of male and female gametes is called sexual reproduction. In humans, the male reproductive organs include a pair of testes, two sperm ducts and a penis. The testes produce the male gametes, called sperms. Millions of sperms are produced by the testes. A sperm is a single cell with a head, a middle piece and a tail; the tail helps it to move. The female reproductive organs are a pair of ovaries, the oviducts (fallopian tubes) and the uterus. The ovaries produce female gametes called ova or eggs. In human beings, a single matured egg is released into the oviduct by one of the ovaries every month. The uterus is the part where development of the baby takes place. Like the sperm, an egg is a single cell.

The first step in the process of reproduction is the fusion of a sperm and an ovum. When sperms come in contact with an egg, one of the sperms may fuse with the egg. This fusion of the egg and the sperm is called fertilisation. During fertilisation, the nuclei of the sperm and the egg fuse to form a single nucleus, resulting in the formation of a fertilised egg called the zygote. The zygote is the beginning of a new individual. It receives some characters from the mother and some from the father.

Fertilisation which takes place inside the female body is called internal fertilisation; it occurs in humans, cows, dogs and hens. Many animals, such as frogs and fish, show external fertilisation, where the fusion of the male and female gamete takes place outside the body of the female. During spring or the rainy season, frogs and toads move to ponds and slow-flowing streams. The female frog lays hundreds of eggs in water. Frog eggs are not covered by a shell and are comparatively delicate; a layer of jelly holds them together and protects them. The male deposits sperms over the eggs, and each sperm swims randomly in water with its long tail. Because many eggs and sperms are exposed to water movement, wind and rainfall and may be eaten by other animals, these animals produce a large number of eggs and sperms to ensure that at least some are fertilised.

Test-tube baby is a term used when a woman's oviducts are blocked. The doctors collect a freshly released egg and sperms and keep them together for a few hours for in vitro fertilisation (IVF), meaning fertilisation outside the body. The zygote is allowed to develop for about a week and is then placed in the mother's uterus, where complete development takes place.

The zygote divides repeatedly to give rise to a ball of cells. The cells then begin to form groups that develop into different tissues and organs of the body. This developing structure is called an embryo. The embryo gets embedded in the wall of the uterus for further development. The stage of the embryo in which all the body parts can be identified is called a foetus. When the development of the foetus is complete, the mother gives birth to the baby.

Animals such as humans, cows and dogs, which give birth to young ones, are called viviparous animals. Animals such as hens, frogs, lizards and butterflies, which lay eggs, are called oviparous animals. In hens, the zygote divides repeatedly as it travels down the oviduct, and protective layers and a hard shell form around it. The hen then lays the egg, and the embryo takes about three weeks to develop into a chick while the hen sits on the eggs to provide warmth.

The transformation of a larva into an adult through drastic changes is called metamorphosis. A frog's life cycle passes through egg, tadpole and adult stages. A silkworm passes through egg, larva (caterpillar), pupa and adult moth stages. In humans, body parts similar to those of adults are present from birth, so there is no metamorphosis.

The type of reproduction in which only a single parent is involved is called asexual reproduction. In Hydra, one or more bulges called buds arise from the body; each bud grows into a new individual and detaches from the parent. This type of asexual reproduction, in which new individuals develop from buds, is called budding. Amoeba is a single-celled organism that reproduces by binary fission: its nucleus divides into two nuclei, and then its body divides into two, each part receiving a nucleus. Two Amoebae are produced from one parent. The type of asexual reproduction in which an animal reproduces by dividing into two individuals is called binary fission.

Dolly the sheep was the first mammal to be cloned, by Ian Wilmut and his colleagues at the Roslin Institute in Scotland. Cloning is the production of an exact copy of a cell, any other living part or a complete organism. Dolly was born on 5 July 1996 and was created from a cell of the mammary gland of a female Finn Dorset sheep; it was genetically identical to that donor sheep.
