Reaching the Age of Adolescence. Human beings, like many other animals, can reproduce only after a certain age. The period of life when the body undergoes changes leading to reproductive maturity is called adolescence. Adolescence begins around the age of 11 and lasts up to 18 or 19 years. Since this period covers the teens (thirteen to eighteen or nineteen years of age), adolescents are also called teenagers. In girls, adolescence may begin a year or two earlier than in boys, and the period of adolescence varies from person to person.

The human body undergoes several changes during adolescence. These changes mark the onset of puberty. The most important change of puberty is that boys and girls become capable of reproduction. Puberty ends when an adolescent reaches reproductive maturity.

The most visible change during puberty is a sudden increase in height. At first the long bones, the bones of the arms and legs, elongate and make a person tall. Initially girls grow faster than boys, but by about 18 years of age both reach their maximum height. The rate of growth in height varies in different individuals. Height depends on the genes inherited from parents, and it is very important to eat the right kind of food during these growing years so that the bones, muscles and other parts of the body get adequate nourishment.

The body shape also changes. Boys develop broader shoulders and wider chests than girls. In girls, the region below the waist becomes wider. In boys, the muscles of the body grow more prominently than in girls.

At puberty, the voice box or larynx begins to grow. Boys develop a larger voice box, and the growing voice box in boys can be seen as a protruding part of the throat called the Adam's apple. In girls the larynx is hardly visible because of its small size. Generally girls have a high-pitched voice, whereas boys have a deeper voice. In adolescent boys, the voice sometimes becomes hoarse because the muscles of the growing voice box go out of control.

During puberty the secretion of sweat glands and sebaceous (oil) glands increases. Many young people get acne and pimples on the face at this time because of the increased activity of these glands.

At puberty, the male sex organs, the testes and penis, develop completely, and the testes begin to produce sperms. In girls, the ovaries enlarge and eggs begin to mature, and ovaries start releasing mature eggs. Adolescence is also a period of change in a person's way of thinking. Adolescents are more independent than before and are also self-conscious. Intellectual development takes place, and they tend to spend considerable time thinking. They should not feel insecure about these changes, which are a natural part of growing up.

Secondary sexual characters are features that help to distinguish the male from the female. In girls, breasts begin to develop, and in boys facial hair, that is the moustache and beard, begins to grow. Boys also develop hair on the chest. In both boys and girls, hair grows under the arms and in the region above the thighs or the pubic region.

The changes that occur at adolescence are controlled by hormones. Hormones are chemical substances secreted by endocrine glands, also called the endocrine system. The male hormone, testosterone, is released by the testes at the onset of puberty and brings about changes in boys, such as the growth of facial hair. Once puberty is reached in girls, the ovaries begin to produce the female hormone, estrogen, which makes the breasts develop. The production of these hormones is under the control of another hormone secreted by an endocrine gland called the pituitary gland. Endocrine glands release hormones into the bloodstream to reach a particular body part called the target site. Other endocrine glands include the thyroid, the adrenals and the pancreas. The thyroid gland produces the hormone thyroxine; if the thyroid gland does not produce thyroxine in sufficient quantities, a person may suffer from goitre, a disease in which the thyroid gland is enlarged. The pancreas produces insulin, and a lack of insulin causes diabetes. The adrenal glands secrete adrenalin, which helps the body adjust to stress, such as when a person is very angry or worried.

Hormones also play an important role in the life cycle of insects and frogs. The transformation of a caterpillar into an adult insect and of a tadpole into a frog is controlled by insect hormones and by thyroxine respectively. Thyroxine production requires the presence of iodine in water, so if the water in which tadpoles grow does not contain sufficient iodine, the tadpoles cannot become adults.

In females, the first menstrual flow begins at puberty and is termed menarche. At the age of about 45 to 50 years the menstrual cycle stops, and the stopping of menstruation is termed menopause. Initially, the menstrual cycle may be irregular, and it takes some time to become regular.

Whether a baby will be a boy or a girl is decided by the sex chromosomes. All human beings have 23 pairs of chromosomes in the nucleus of their cells. Two of these are the sex chromosomes, named X and Y. A female has two X chromosomes, while a male has one X and one Y chromosome. The unfertilised egg always has one X chromosome, while sperms are of two kinds: one kind has an X chromosome and the other has a Y chromosome. When a sperm carrying an X chromosome fertilises the egg, the zygote develops into a female child; when a sperm carrying a Y chromosome fertilises the egg, the zygote develops into a male child. So the sex of the child is determined by the sperm from the father.

Adolescence is a time to take care of personal health. A balanced diet, which includes proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins and minerals in the right proportion, is important; milk, sprouts, pulses, fruits and green vegetables are good choices, and iron-rich foods such as leafy vegetables, jaggery, meat, citrus fruit, Indian gooseberry (amla) and nuts help build blood. Personal hygiene, such as bathing daily and keeping the body clean, prevents infections. Regular physical exercise and play keep the body fit. Adolescents should say no to drugs, which are addictive and harm health. A virus called HIV, which causes AIDS, can pass from an infected person to a healthy person through sharing syringes, from an infected mother to her infant through her milk, or through sexual contact.
