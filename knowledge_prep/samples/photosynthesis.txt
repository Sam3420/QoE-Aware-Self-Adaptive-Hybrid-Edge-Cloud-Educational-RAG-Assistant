Hello everyone, and welcome to today's lesson on photosynthesis. Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food. The word comes from two Greek words: photo, which means light, and synthesis, which means putting together. So photosynthesis means putting things together using light. Plants are called producers because they make their own food, while animals, including us, are consumers because we eat plants or other animals.

What does a plant need for photosynthesis? It needs four things. First, sunlight, which gives the energy. Second, water, which the roots absorb from the soil and send up through the stem to the leaves. Third, carbon dioxide, a gas that enters the leaf from the air through tiny holes called stomata. Fourth, chlorophyll, the green pigment inside the leaf that captures the light energy. Without chlorophyll the plant cannot trap sunlight, which is why the parts of a plant that are not green usually do not carry out photosynthesis.

Where does photosynthesis happen? It happens mainly in the leaves, inside small structures called chloroplasts. Each chloroplast contains chlorophyll. The leaf is flat and broad so that it can catch as much sunlight as possible, and it has many stomata on its lower surface so that gases can move in and out.

Now let us write the word equation. Carbon dioxide plus water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, gives glucose plus oxygen. The chemical equation is six CO2 plus six H2O gives C6H12O6 plus six O2. Glucose is a simple sugar. It is the food of the plant. Oxygen is released into the air as a by-product, and this is the oxygen that we and other animals breathe.

How do plants convert sunlight into chemical energy? Photosynthesis happens in two stages. In the first stage, called the light-dependent reactions, chlorophyll absorbs light energy. This energy is used to split water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen. The oxygen is released. The energy is stored for a short time in energy-carrier molecules called ATP and NADPH. In the second stage, called the Calvin cycle or the light-independent reactions, the plant uses the stored energy from ATP and NADPH to combine carbon dioxide with hydrogen and build glucose. In this way light energy from the sun is converted into chemical energy stored in the bonds of glucose.

What does the plant do with glucose? Some glucose is used immediately in respiration to release energy for growth. Some is converted into starch and stored in the leaves, roots, stems and seeds. Potatoes and rice grains, for example, are full of stored starch. Some glucose is used to make cellulose, which builds the cell walls.

Which factors affect the rate of photosynthesis? Light intensity is the first factor: more light usually means faster photosynthesis, up to a limit. Carbon dioxide concentration is the second: more carbon dioxide increases the rate, until another factor becomes limiting. Temperature is the third: photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes, so it works best at a moderate temperature, around twenty-five to thirty-five degrees Celsius, and slows down when it is too cold or too hot. Water supply also matters, because when a plant is short of water it closes its stomata and less carbon dioxide can enter.

A simple experiment to test a leaf for starch: take a leaf that has been in sunlight, boil it in water, then in alcohol to remove the chlorophyll, rinse it, and add a few drops of iodine solution. If the leaf turns blue-black, starch is present, which shows that photosynthesis has taken place. If you cover part of the leaf with black paper for a day, that part will not turn blue-black, which proves that light is needed for photosynthesis.

Why is photosynthesis important for us? It produces the oxygen we breathe. It is the starting point of almost every food chain, because all the energy in our food comes originally from sunlight captured by plants. It also removes carbon dioxide from the air, which helps to reduce global warming. That is why farmers, and all of us, depend on healthy green plants. In our next lesson we will study respiration, which is the process that releases the energy stored in glucose.
