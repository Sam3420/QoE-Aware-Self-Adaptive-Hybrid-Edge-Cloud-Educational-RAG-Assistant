\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This project addressed the question of how an educational assistant can keep giving correct, grounded and
timely help when the network and the device change, without sending every question to the cloud. It
built a QoE-aware framework that answers questions from the lesson notes and the chosen video's transcript
(RAG with corrective retrieval), runs either a small model on the learner's laptop or a large model in the
cloud according to a runtime policy, measures every session as quality of experience and resource
efficiency, and chooses the next session's policy from similar past sessions. A proactive coach sets
concept-level goals from a pre-test, suggests the next step after every answer and reflects after a
parallel post-test. The case study covers five NCERT Class~X Biology chapters with 298 checked items.

The system-level evaluation, run on real answers and timings with measured and emulated network
conditions, supports the following conclusions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The edge model answers in about 0.7~s and the cloud model in about 1.3~s, but the cloud is far more
    accurate (100\% against 54\% on the keyed items), so the cloud is the better choice whenever it is
    reachable, even at 2~s of round-trip time.
  \item Corrective retrieval improves the grounding of edge answers on learner-style questions
    (faithfulness 0.70 to 0.80), and a confidence-based cascade (P5) recovers 92\% accuracy with half
    the cloud calls.
  \item Experience-based policy selection outperforms every fixed configuration under a changing network
    in its revised form, QAPS: utility 0.812 against 0.794 for the best fixed policy and 0.818 for the
    hindsight oracle, with no failed cloud calls, half the slow answers and 44\% less energy than
    always-cloud. The selector as first designed did not outperform a fixed cloud policy; the analysis
    of why (indistinguishable outage and poor-network states, evidence starvation, unsafe exploration)
    led directly to the four changes in QAPS.
  \item The coach's decisions cost under a millisecond; speech recognition is fast and accurate in Hindi;
    the conclusion that QAPS performs best holds under data-derived (EWM) weights and across utility
    weightings.
\end{itemize}
Objectives O1, O2, O3 and O5 are met at system level. Objective O4 is met for the mechanisms
(goal-setting, coaching, recommendation) but not for its outcome criterion, learning gain, which requires
learners. The results therefore establish that the framework is technically effective; they do not yet
show that it improves learning.

\section{Future work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{User study (next phase).} A within-subject study with Class~X learners comparing coaching
    \emph{on} and \emph{off} (counterbalanced test forms) to measure learning gain, satisfaction and the
    full QoE including the EQ and UX groups. Depends on ethics approval and completion of the content
    review.
  \item \textbf{Complete the content review.} Close the 28 open flags, collect the two human concept-tag
    labels to measure tagging accuracy, and review generated practice items before they are shown.
  \item \textbf{Validate the network model.} Repeat a subset of the matrix with live injected delay and
    loss, and measure the poor and outage profiles on real networks.
  \item \textbf{Safer edge answers.} Decline outside the model when retrieval stays below the threshold,
    so that off-topic questions are not answered by the edge model.
  \item \textbf{Finer adaptation.} Per-question routing, bandwidth and jitter in the state, recency
    weighting of experiences, and a larger action set (temperature, chunk size, speech parameters), as
    in the original design.
  \item \textbf{Speech.} Choose the recogniser by language and use a faster Hindi synthesiser.
  \item \textbf{Make QAPS the default} after the user study confirms its behaviour with real sessions.
\end{enumerate}
