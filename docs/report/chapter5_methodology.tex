\chapter{Proposed Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\section{Design (architecture) or conceptual framework}
\label{sec:design}

The proposed framework is a QoE-aware, self-adaptive learning assistant that runs on the learner's own
device (the \emph{edge}) and uses the cloud only when it pays off. It combines three mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Grounded question answering.} Answers are generated from the lesson notes of the topic and
        the transcript of the video the learner is watching, using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
        with corrective retrieval (CRAG).
  \item \textbf{Hybrid edge--cloud execution.} Each answer is produced either by a small language model on
        the device or by a large language model in the cloud, according to the runtime policy of the
        session.
  \item \textbf{Experience-based adaptation.} Every session is measured (quality of experience and resource
        use) and stored; the next session's policy is chosen from similar past sessions.
\end{enumerate}
A \emph{proactive coach} runs alongside: it sets learning goals from a pre-test, suggests the next step
after every answer instead of waiting for the learner to ask, and reflects on the outcome after a
post-test. RAG, CRAG, FAISS and the speech models are building blocks; the contribution is their
integration with QoE- and resource-aware runtime adaptation, and the policy selector of
Section~\ref{sec:selection}.

A subject enters the framework only through a \emph{content package} (Section~\ref{sec:content}). To test
the architecture end to end, the scope is one case study: five Biology chapters of the NCERT Class~X
\emph{Science} textbook, chosen because the content is bounded, answers can be checked against a
textbook, and suitable videos exist.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the components. Everything the learner touches, the local models and
the database run on one laptop; the cloud language models, YouTube and the tracing service are external.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig5_1_architecture.pdf}
  \caption{Architecture of the framework. Arrows show the main data flow: the agent drives the coach,
    retrieval and the recommender; retrieved evidence goes to the router, which answers on the edge or in
    the cloud according to the policy chosen by the adaptive policy engine (QAPS) from the experience
    memory, which the monitoring component fills after every session.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\begin{description}
  \item[Learner interface.] A Streamlit web interface with a FastAPI backend. Questions are typed or
    spoken; speech-to-text uses SraVaani-1.0 (65 Indian languages and dialects plus English) with
    distil-whisper as an English fallback, and answers can be read aloud with Kokoro-82M.
  \item[LangGraph agent.] One state graph runs a learning session as a sequence of nodes. It pauses
    whenever the learner must act (an \emph{interrupt}) and resumes on the reply; its state is
    checkpointed in SQLite, so a session survives page reloads.
  \item[Proactive coach.] A per-concept mastery tracker, a goal planner, next-step rules, a pool of
    practice (formative) questions, a parallel post-test and a reflection step (Section~\ref{sec:coach}).
  \item[Hybrid RAG with CRAG.] Lesson notes and the chosen video's transcript are chunked, embedded with
    \texttt{nomic-embed-text} and indexed in FAISS; CRAG grades the retrieved evidence and corrects the
    query when it is weak (Section~\ref{sec:rag}).
  \item[Router and language models.] The router executes the session's policy: the edge model
    \texttt{gemma3:1b} (served by Ollama on the laptop GPU), the cloud model \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} on Groq,
    or the edge model with escalation to the cloud; \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} checks the grounding of edge
    answers for policy P5.
  \item[Video recommender.] Searches and ranks YouTube videos for the session's goals and keeps only
    videos with a usable caption track (Section~\ref{sec:video}).
  \item[Monitoring.] Measures network delay, CPU and memory load, energy, latency, retrieval and answer
    quality, and computes QoE, SRE, utility and reward per session (Section~\ref{sec:qoe}); every node and
    model call can be traced in Langfuse.
  \item[Experience memory and adaptive policy engine.] Each completed session is stored as an experience
    in SQLite with its context vector in FAISS; the engine (QAPS) chooses the next session's policy from
    similar experiences (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).
  \item[Content packages.] The subject-specific input: concepts, test items, practice items and videos.
\end{description}

\subsection{Key concepts}
\label{sec:concepts}

\begin{description}
  \item[Concept.] A unit of a topic (for example \emph{dominant and recessive traits}). Every test item,
    practice question and learner question is tagged with exactly one concept.
  \item[Learning goal.] A concept the session aims to improve; a session has two to four goals.
  \item[Runtime policy.] A predefined configuration $a=(\text{route},k,r)$: where answers are generated
    (edge, cloud, or edge with escalation), how many chunks are retrieved ($k$) and how many corrective
    retries CRAG may make ($r$). The five policies P1--P5 (Table~\ref{tab:policies}) are the actions
    among which the adaptive engine chooses.
  \item[Context (state).] The measurable situation at the start of a session,
    $s=(\mathit{rtt},\mathit{cpu},\mathit{mem},\mathit{competency},\mathit{QoE}_{\text{last}})$.
  \item[Experience.] A completed session $(s,a,R,s')$, stored with the utility predicted before the
    session and the utility observed after it.
\end{description}

\subsection{Conceptual model: two closed loops}
\label{sec:loops}

The framework runs two loops that meet in every session. The \textbf{runtime loop} decides \emph{how}
questions are answered:
\[
  s_t \;\rightarrow\; a_t \;\rightarrow\; \text{session} \;\rightarrow\; (\mathit{QoE},\mathit{SRE})
  \;\rightarrow\; R_t \;\rightarrow\; \text{experience memory} \;\rightarrow\; a_{t+1}.
\]
The \textbf{learner loop} decides \emph{what} the learner works on:
\[
  \text{pre-test} \rightarrow \text{goals} \rightarrow \text{coach steps} \rightarrow \text{post-test}
  \rightarrow \text{reflection} \rightarrow \text{next session}.
\]
The coach's goals shape the video and the questions; the policy determines how those questions are
answered.

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(A1)] One video is the main resource at a time; answers are grounded in its transcript and the
    topic's lesson notes.
  \item[(A2)] The edge model is cheaper, private and works offline but is weaker; the cloud model is
    stronger but depends on the network.
  \item[(A3)] The conditions that matter for the choice of policy (network delay, device load, learner
    competency) can be measured at the start of a session.
  \item[(A4)] A session is the unit of adaptation: one policy per session, judged at its end.
  \item[(A5)] One concept per question is fine enough for mastery tracking and coaching.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed approach and procedure}
\label{sec:procedure}

The procedure has seven parts: preparing content (\ref{sec:content}), running a session with the coach
(\ref{sec:coach}), answering questions (\ref{sec:rag}), measuring the session (\ref{sec:qoe}), selecting
the policy (\ref{sec:selection}), recommending videos (\ref{sec:video}) and verifying the method
(\ref{sec:evalproc}). The implementation uses Python~3.11.9, LangGraph, FastAPI, Streamlit, SQLite and
FAISS; the hardware and libraries are listed in Tables~4.3--4.5.

\subsection{Content preparation}
\label{sec:content}

\textbf{Input:} source material for a topic. \textbf{Output:} a content package with a concept vocabulary
(8--10 concepts with aliases), a 15-item pre-test bank, a 15-item \emph{parallel} post-test (each item has
the same concept and difficulty as one pre-test item but different wording), a separate pool of practice
questions, and candidate videos. Every item has four options, a key, an explanation, one concept, a
difficulty and a reference to the supporting section and page. Table~\ref{tab:packages} lists the
packages of the case study.

    How do Organisms Reproduce?    & 10 & 15 & 15 & 30 & 5 \\

Packages are drafted with LLM assistance and then checked three times: (i) a validation script checks
grounding in the cited page, copying from the source, answer-position balance and concept coverage;
(ii) a \emph{blind check} lets a second model answer and tag every item without the key, and
disagreements are only flagged; (iii) people decide on the flagged items. The textbook text stays on the
local machine; packages store only section and page references.

\subsection{Session workflow and the proactive coach}
\label{sec:coach}

\textbf{Input:} a student ID and a topic. \textbf{Output:} grounded answers, coach suggestions, pre- and
post-test scores, a learning gain, a reflection and one stored experience. Figure~\ref{fig:workflow}
shows the twelve steps of a session; Figure~\ref{fig:coach} shows the coach in detail.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig5_2_session_workflow.pdf}
  \caption{Workflow of one learning session. Steps 1--4 prepare the session and fix its policy, steps
    5--8 form the learning loop, and steps 9--12 close the session and store the experience used by the
    next session's policy choice (dashed arrow).}
  \label{fig:workflow}
\end{figure}

All coaching decisions use the Laplace (rule-of-succession) estimate of the mastery of concept $c$,
\begin{equation}
  m_c = \frac{n^{\text{correct}}_c + 1}{n^{\text{attempts}}_c + 2},
  \label{eq:mastery}
\end{equation}
which is $0.5$ for an unseen concept and moves towards the observed accuracy as answers accumulate; unlike
the raw accuracy it does not jump to 0 or 1 after one answer.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig5_3_proactive_coach.pdf}
  \caption{The proactive coach in three stages. Stage~2 applies the first matching rule after every
    answer; concepts still weak at the end are carried into the next session.}
  \label{fig:coach}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Stage 1: pre-test and goals.} A five-item pre-test, one item per concept; a returning learner
first gets the items missed last time. The score sets a competency band (advanced $\geq 0.8$,
intermediate $\geq 0.5$, otherwise beginner) and every answer updates Eq.~\eqref{eq:mastery}. The coach
then sets two to four goals in priority order: concepts still weak after the previous session, concepts
missed in this pre-test, other concepts with mastery below 0.5, and, only to reach two goals, concepts
never practised. The goals also form the video query.

\paragraph{Stage 2: next-step suggestions.} After every answer the question is tagged with a concept (by
name or alias, otherwise by embedding similarity above 0.6) and mastery is updated. The coach then picks
one next step by the first matching rule:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{formative check} -- the question concerned a goal concept with mastery below 0.5;
  \item \emph{another video} -- the current video does not cover one of the goal concepts;
  \item \emph{review} -- the last answer was escalated, failed the grounding check or was weakly retrieved;
  \item \emph{post-test} -- every goal concept has reached mastery 0.75;
  \item \emph{follow-up} -- otherwise, on the next goal not yet asked about, or the weakest goal.
\end{enumerate}
The learner accepts or skips the suggestion; a suggestion skipped twice in a row is muted, and a session
has at most ten questions. Practice questions come from the concept's pool and are never test items, so
practice cannot leak the post-test. When a pool runs short, the LLM generates a new item that is checked
automatically (novel, mapped to the right concept) and shown without human review. Rules rather than a
learned model are used because they are transparent, need no training data and give a clear reference.
The coaching mode is stored with each session; mode \emph{off} gives the reactive, learner-asks-only
assistant.

\paragraph{Stage 3: post-test and reflection.} The post-test presents each pre-test item's parallel item,
so the two tests share no items and an improvement reflects the concept rather than memory of the
question. Learning gain is the Hake normalised gain
\begin{equation}
  g = \frac{\mathit{post}-\mathit{pre}}{1-\mathit{pre}}.
  \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
A reflection shows the goals reached and the mastery of each goal before and after; concepts still weak
are saved to the learner's profile for Stage~1 of the next session.

\subsection{Hybrid RAG, CRAG and edge--cloud routing}
\label{sec:rag}

\textbf{Input:} a question and the session's policy $a=(\text{route},k,r)$. \textbf{Output:} an answer
with its route, retrieval score and, for P5, grounding score.

\paragraph{Knowledge preparation.} The lesson notes and the chosen video's transcript are cleaned, split
into 120-word chunks with a 25-word overlap, embedded with \texttt{nomic-embed-text} and stored in a FAISS
index that is reused when the same video is chosen again.

\paragraph{Corrective retrieval.} For a question $q$ CRAG retrieves the $k$ most similar chunks and takes
the highest similarity as the retrieval score $S(q)$. Chunks below 0.50 are discarded, and the evidence is
accepted when
\begin{equation}
  S(q) \geq \tau, \qquad \tau = 0.65.
  \label{eq:crag}
\end{equation}
Otherwise, while the retry budget $r$ lasts, the query is rewritten (filler words removed, topic terms
added) and retrieval repeated; the attempts are merged. If the evidence stays below $\tau$, the model is
instructed to say that the lesson does not cover the question rather than invent an answer.

\paragraph{Routing.} The router executes the policy (Table~\ref{tab:policies}); it does not learn. P5 is a
confidence-aware cascade that starts on the edge: a retrieval score in the borderline band $[0.55,0.65)$
sends the question directly to the cloud; otherwise the edge model answers, \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} scores the
share of the answer's claims supported by the retrieved chunks, and an answer scoring below $0.75$ is
replaced by a cloud answer. If a route fails (for example the cloud is unreachable), the router falls
back to the edge model and avoids the failed route for 30~s.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Predefined runtime policies (the actions of the adaptive engine).}
  \label{tab:policies}
  \begin{tabular}{llccl}
    \toprule
    Policy & Route & $k$ & $r$ & Intended situation \\
    \midrule
    P1 edge-light      & edge     & 3 & 0 & offline or weak network; cheapest (default) \\
    P2 edge-thorough   & edge     & 5 & 1 & offline, question needs more context \\
    P3 cloud-light     & cloud    & 3 & 0 & good network; accurate and quick \\
    P4 cloud-thorough  & cloud    & 5 & 1 & good network, harder questions \\
    P5 edge-escalate   & escalate & 5 & 1 & edge first, cloud only when evidence or answer is weak \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The policies are designed, not learned. Three parameters with the largest effect on the QoE--resource
trade-off were selected (route, $k$, $r$), each with two levels to keep the action space small enough
to learn from few sessions; moving $k$ and $r$ together gives a ``light'' and a ``thorough'' option per
route, and P5 follows the model-cascade idea of using the large model only when needed.

\subsection{QoE, SRE, utility and reward}
\label{sec:qoe}

\textbf{Input:} the measurements of one session. \textbf{Output:} QoE, SRE, utility $U$ and reward $R$.
Every measurement is normalised to $[0,1]$ with higher meaning better (costs such as latency are
inverted). Quality of experience combines four groups,
\begin{align}
  \mathit{EQ} &= \alpha_1 \mathit{LG} + \alpha_2 \mathit{PT} + \alpha_3 \mathit{RA} + \alpha_4 \mathit{LS}, &
  \mathit{AQ} &= \beta_1 \mathit{RQ} + \beta_2 \mathit{LQ} + \beta_3 \mathit{SQ} + \beta_4 \mathit{TQ}, \nonumber\\
  \mathit{NQ} &= \gamma_1 \mathit{PL} + \gamma_2 \mathit{NR} + \gamma_3 \mathit{RU} + \gamma_4 \mathit{AV}, &
  \mathit{UX} &= \delta_1 \mathit{SR} + \delta_2 \mathit{FS} + \delta_3 \mathit{CR}, \nonumber\\
  \mathit{QoE} &= w_{EQ}\,\mathit{EQ} + w_{AQ}\,\mathit{AQ} + w_{NQ}\,\mathit{NQ} + w_{UX}\,\mathit{UX}.
  \label{eq:qoe}
\end{align}
The educational group uses learning gain (LG), post-test score (PT), recommendation acceptance (RA) and
learner satisfaction (LS, the mean star rating given to recommended videos). The answer-quality group
uses retrieval quality (RQ, the CRAG score), answer quality (LQ), speech-recognition quality (SQ) and
speech-synthesis quality (TQ). The network group uses pipeline latency (PL, 1 at $\leq$1.5~s and 0 at
$\geq$15~s), network reliability (NR, share of successful network operations), resource utilisation (RU)
and availability (AV, share of answers from a language model). The user-experience group uses the
session rating (SR), feedback sentiment (FS) and completion (CR).

System resource efficiency measures how much of a per-session budget was used,
\begin{equation}
  \mathit{SRE} = \eta_1\Bigl(1-\frac{u_{cpu}}{u^{\max}_{cpu}}\Bigr) + \eta_2\Bigl(1-\frac{u_{mem}}{u^{\max}_{mem}}\Bigr)
  + \eta_3\Bigl(1-\frac{E}{E^{\max}}\Bigr) + \eta_4\Bigl(1-\frac{B}{B^{\max}}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:sre}
\end{equation}
with each term clipped to $[0,1]$, $B$ the bytes transferred ($B^{\max}=50$~MB) and the energy $E$
($E^{\max}=2$~Wh) estimated with a linear CPU power model
\begin{equation}
  E = \bigl(P_{\text{idle}} + u_{cpu}(P_{\max}-P_{\text{idle}})\bigr)\,t_{\text{answer}},
  \qquad P_{\text{idle}}=8~\text{W},\; P_{\max}=45~\text{W};
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
in the benchmarks, the GPU board power measured through NVML above its idle level (6.2~W) is added.
Because SRE alone would reward doing nothing, it is always combined with QoE:
\begin{equation}
  U = \lambda_1\,\mathit{QoE} + \lambda_2\,\mathit{SRE}, \qquad
  R = \mu_1\,(\mathit{QoE}'-\mathit{QoE}) + \mu_2\,(\mathit{SRE}'-\mathit{SRE}),
  \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_1=\mu_1=0.7$ and $\lambda_2=\mu_2=0.3$, so that the learner's experience dominates while
resource use still counts. All weights are fixed (Table~\ref{tab:weights}); every group is non-negative
and sums to one. When a measurement is unavailable, the weights of the available ones in its group are
renormalised and the share of weight backed by data (\emph{coverage}) is reported, so a score never
claims more evidence than it has.

\subsection{Adaptive policy selection}
\label{sec:selection}

\textbf{Input:} the context of a new session and the experience memory. \textbf{Output:} a policy $a^*$
and its predicted utility. Selection is \emph{case-based}: no model is trained, and every decision can be
traced to the past sessions it was based on. Two procedures are defined and compared in
Chapter~\ref{ch:results}: the baseline selector, which implements the design as first specified, and
QAPS (\emph{QoE-Aware Policy Selector}), its revision.

\paragraph{Utility estimate (both procedures).} The context is embedded as a vector
$\mathbf{x}(s)\in[0,1]^5$ and completed sessions are indexed in an exact FAISS index. The expected utility
of policy $a$ in state $s$ is estimated from past sessions $i$ that executed $a$, weighted by a Gaussian
kernel of their distance $d_i$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat U(s,a) = \frac{\sum_{i} w_i\,U_i}{\sum_{i} w_i}, \qquad
  w_i = \exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{d_i^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigr), \quad \sigma = 0.25,
  \label{eq:estimate}
\end{equation}
where $U_i$ is the utility observed after session $i$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:utility}). This is the
Nadaraya--Watson kernel-regression estimator of $\mathbb{E}[U\mid s,a]$: it assumes that similar
situations give similar outcomes and converges to the true expected utility as experience accumulates. A
policy receives an estimate only when $\sum_i w_i \geq 0.1$, so evidence from very different situations
is not trusted. The prediction $\hat U(s,a^*)$ is stored next to the utility later observed, so the
accuracy of the predictions can be measured.

\paragraph{Baseline selector.} The RTT is divided by 2{,}000~ms and clipped; the $K=5$ nearest past
sessions of \emph{any} policy are retrieved, each policy is estimated from those among them that used it,
and the highest estimate is chosen (ties to the default P1). With probability $\varepsilon=0.2$ a random
other policy is tried instead, and with fewer than three stored sessions the default P1 is used.

\paragraph{QAPS.} The benchmarks of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} showed three weaknesses of the baseline under
changing networks, and QAPS changes four things (Figure~\ref{fig:qaps}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Network state.} The RTT probe waits up to 4~s instead of 1.5~s (one HTTPS probe is about
    four round trips, so a poor network already timed out), and the RTT is encoded on a log scale up to the
    failure value $\mathit{rtt}_{\text{fail}}=5{,}000$~ms,
    \begin{equation}
      x_{rtt} = \frac{\ln(1+\mathit{rtt})}{\ln(1+\mathit{rtt}_{\text{fail}})},
      \label{eq:logrtt}
    \end{equation}
    so that a poor network (about 2{,}400~ms) and an unreachable cloud are different states; the clipped
    linear feature mapped both to 1.0. A logarithmic scale also matches the observation in QoE research
    that perceived quality depends roughly on the logarithm of delay.
  \item \textbf{Per-policy neighbours.} Each policy is estimated from \emph{its own} $k$ nearest past
    sessions (from the 200 nearest overall). In the baseline, a policy used often fills the shared top-5,
    the other policies then have no evidence, and only random exploration ever tries them again.
  \item \textbf{Exploration by uncertainty.} The choice maximises an upper-confidence score,
    \begin{equation}
      \mathrm{score}(s,a) = \hat U(s,a) + \frac{c}{\sqrt{1+W_a(s)}}, \qquad W_a(s)=\sum_i w_i, \quad c=0.05,
      \label{eq:ucb}
    \end{equation}
    where $W_a$ acts as the effective number of similar samples; the bonus shrinks as $1/\sqrt{W_a}$, like
    the width of a confidence interval of a mean (UCB principle). A policy with no evidence near the
    current state is tried first, and a small random exploration $\varepsilon=0.05$ remains.
  \item \textbf{Availability guard.} While the probe fails, only edge policies (P1, P2) are candidates:
    a cloud policy would make every answer wait for the cloud timeout before falling back.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figures/fig5_4_qaps.pdf}
  \caption{Policy selection by QAPS at the start of every session. Side boxes are the special cases
    (cold start, untried policy, random exploration); the dashed arrow is the experience added after the
    session.}
  \label{fig:qaps}
\end{figure}

\noindent\textbf{Procedure QAPS} (input: context $s$, memory $\mathcal{D}$, policies $\mathcal{A}$, default
$a_0=\text{P1}$; output: $a^*$ and $\hat U(s,a^*)$)
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Measure $\mathit{rtt}$ with a 4~s probe (on failure $\mathit{rtt}\leftarrow\mathit{rtt}_{\text{fail}}$),
    CPU, memory, competency and last QoE; build $\mathbf{x}(s)$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:logrtt}.
  \item $\mathcal{C}\leftarrow\{\text{P1},\text{P2}\}$ if the probe failed, otherwise $\mathcal{A}$.
  \item If $|\mathcal{D}|<3$: return $a_0$ (cold start).
  \item For each $a\in\mathcal{C}$: take the $k$ nearest experiences of $a$ to $\mathbf{x}(s)$ and compute
    $\hat U(s,a)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:estimate}) and $W_a(s)$.
  \item If some $a\in\mathcal{C}$ has no estimate: return it (the default first, if it is among them).
  \item With probability $\varepsilon=0.05$: return a random $a\in\mathcal{C}$.
  \item Return $a^*=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{score}(s,a)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ucb}); ties go to $a_0$.
\end{enumerate}
After the session, its observed utility and $(s,a^*,R,s')$ are stored as a new experience. The baseline
selector is the same procedure without steps~2 and~5, with a linear RTT feature, the shared top-5
neighbours in step~4, plain argmax in step~7 and $\varepsilon=0.2$.

\subsection{Video recommendation}
\label{sec:video}

\textbf{Input:} topic, goal concepts, competency band and language. \textbf{Output:} a ranked list of
usable videos. The query combines the topic, the goals and a level hint. Candidates come from the YouTube
Data API v3 (search and metadata only, SafeSearch strict, quota tracked, results cached), pass hard
filters, and are ranked in two stages: titles and descriptions are scored by semantic similarity to the
goals, then transcripts of the best candidates are fetched and scored for how well they cover the goals.
Three strategies trade speed for evidence: \emph{R1 metadata-fast} fetches no transcripts, \emph{R2
balanced} up to four and \emph{R3 transcript-deep} up to eight. Because questions are answered from the
transcript, a video is offered only if it has a caption track in the learner's language; all candidates'
tracks are checked in one parallel round (at most 6~s), alongside the transcript fetches, and the results
are cached for 24~h. The strategy is chosen from the same context vector by a simpler selector: the mean
reward of the five nearest past recommendation runs with $\varepsilon$-greedy exploration, where the
reward is a provisional signal from video acceptance and ratings.

\subsection{Evaluation procedure}
\label{sec:evalproc}

The method is verified with reproducible benchmarks (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}) that run the agent's real
question-answering code. No learners are involved; Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the results.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig6_1_evaluation_pipeline.pdf}
  \caption{Evaluation pipeline. Real answers and measurements from the answer matrix are combined with
    network profiles in a trace replay that runs the real policy engine; all scores use the system's own
    metric code.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Answer matrix.} Each question is answered once under every policy and control variant
    through the real nodes (retrieval, CRAG, router, models, P5 check). For each answer the benchmark
    records correctness against the key, retrieval score and retries, route, latency per step, tokens, CPU
    and memory load, GPU power (NVML), energy and bytes sent to the cloud.
  \item \textbf{Network profiles and network model.} Round-trip time, jitter and loss to the cloud API and
    the bandwidth are measured on real networks. An application-level network model adds what a profile
    does to each cloud call that the matrix measured: four round trips per call (DNS, TCP, TLS, request),
    jitter, transfer time at the profile's bandwidth and one retransmission timeout per lost packet. Two
    outages are modelled: \emph{offline} (the call fails at once) and \emph{blackhole} (connected but no
    internet: the call waits the 60~s client timeout). In both, the router falls back to the edge.
  \item \textbf{Trace replay.} A session is five questions of one topic under one network condition. A
    trace of 44 sessions (good $\rightarrow$ poor $\rightarrow$ blackhole $\rightarrow$ moderate
    $\rightarrow$ offline $\rightarrow$ good) is replayed 20 times with different questions. Compared
    strategies: the five fixed policies; an RTT rule (cloud when the probe is under 1~s); random choice;
    the baseline selector and QAPS, each starting with an empty memory (\emph{cold}) or pre-trained on a
    trace over the other half of the questions (\emph{warm}); and an oracle that picks the best policy for
    each session in hindsight. All strategies see the same questions and network draws (paired design).
    Metrics: QoE, SRE, utility $U$, regret ($U_{\text{oracle}}-U$), sessions needed to adapt after a change,
    answers slower than 5~s, cloud failures and prediction error $|\hat U-U|$.
  \item \textbf{Retrieval ablation.} The keyed items are also answered by RAG without the corrective retry
    and without retrieval (closed book); learner-style doubts and off-topic questions measure retry rate,
    declines and \emph{faithfulness}, the share of an answer's claims supported by the retrieved text as
    judged by \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}.
  \item \textbf{Coach, speech, videos and content.} Time and model calls of each coach step and the cost of
    answer scaffolding; word error rate (WER) and latency of speech recognition; latency, quota and usable
    videos of R1--R3; and content statistics from the validation and review records.
  \item \textbf{Weight sensitivity.} The Entropy Weight Method (EWM) is applied to the fixed-policy
    sessions of the replay, separately per weight group. For metric $j$ with min--max-scaled values
    $x_{ij}$ over $n$ sessions,
    \begin{equation}
      p_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij}}{\sum_i x_{ij}}, \qquad
      e_j = -\frac{1}{\ln n}\sum_i p_{ij}\ln p_{ij}, \qquad
      w_j = \frac{1-e_j}{\sum_k (1-e_k)},
      \label{eq:ewm}
    \end{equation}
    so metrics that vary more receive more weight. Only the system-side metrics measured in the benchmarks
    are re-weighted; the replay, including the engines' own decisions, is repeated with these weights,
    and $\lambda_1$ is varied from 0.5 to 0.9. A high EWM weight indicates variation, not importance, so
    EWM is a robustness check, not a replacement for the fixed weights.
  \item \textbf{Statistics.} Paired correctness is compared with the exact McNemar test; strategies with
    the Friedman test followed by pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm correction. Means carry
    bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals, proportions Wilson intervals, and ranking agreement is measured
    with Kendall's $\tau$.
\end{enumerate}
The benchmarks have no learner-side measurements (EQ and UX), so benchmark QoE is renormalised over AQ
and NQ with coverage 0.375. All runs use fixed seeds and work on a copy of the database; the code is
covered by 429 automated tests that run offline. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the parameters.
